% Ticks along the top edge of a horizontal scale, hanging down from
% y = 0, with tick i at x = i times the gap.
% \hticks{first i}{last i}{cm per gap}{label every}{longer tick every}{value at i=0}{value per gap}
\newcommand{\hticks}[7]{%
  \foreach \i in {#1,...,#2}{%
    \pgfmathtruncatemacro{\islab}{ifthenelse(mod(\i,#4)==0,1,0)}%
    \pgfmathtruncatemacro{\ismid}{ifthenelse(mod(\i,#5)==0,1,0)}%
    \ifnum\islab=1
      \draw[tick] ({\i*(#3)},0) -- ++(0,-0.5);
      \pgfmathsetmacro{\val}{#6+\i*(#7)}%
      \node[ticklab,below] at ({\i*(#3)},-0.5) {\pgfmathprintnumber{\val}};
    \else\ifnum\ismid=1
      \draw[tick] ({\i*(#3)},0) -- ++(0,-0.36);
    \else
      \draw[tick] ({\i*(#3)},0) -- ++(0,-0.22);
    \fi\fi}}

\parttitle{2}{Error intervals}

\begin{keyidea}
When we read a scale to the nearest tick, the reading is not the exact
measurement. The true measurement could be up to half of one gap below the
reading, or up to half of one gap above it. On a metre rule marked in
centimetres one gap is 1\,cm, so a reading of 73\,cm is accurate to
$\pm\,0.5$\,cm. The symbol $\pm$ means ``plus or minus''.

\smallskip
The smallest value the true measurement could be is the \textbf{lower bound}.
The true measurement must be less than the \textbf{upper bound}: a length of
exactly 73.5\,cm is halfway between two ticks, and halfway values are rounded
up, so it would be read as 74\,cm.

\smallskip
The \textbf{error interval} gives every value the true measurement could be.
We write it with the inequality symbols $\le$, which means ``is less than or
equal to'', and $<$, which means ``is less than''.
\end{keyidea}
