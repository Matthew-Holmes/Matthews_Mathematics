\begin{frame}{Worked Solution: Retrieve and Connect 1, Question 1}
\hypertarget{work-st1}{}
\small
\textbf{Question:} On numberline A what numbers are marked by $P$ and $Q$?
\awork{Solution: Numberline A runs from $0$ to $10$ with ten equal intervals, so each tick is worth $1$. Point $P$ is $3$ ticks to the right of $0$, so $P=3$. Point $Q$ is $8$ ticks to the right of $0$, so $Q=8$.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Worked Solution: Retrieve and Connect 1, Question 2}
\small
\textbf{Question:} On numberline B what numbers are marked by $S$ and $T$?
\awork{Solution: Numberline B runs from $0$ to $1$ in tenths, so each tick is worth $0.1$. Point $S$ is $3$ ticks from $0$, so $S=0.3$. Point $T$ is $9$ ticks from $0$, so $T=0.9$.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Worked Solution: Retrieve and Connect 1, Question 3}
\small
\textbf{Question:} Mark $6$ on Line~A and $0.45$ on Line~B.
\awork{Solution: On Line A, each tick is worth $1$, so $6$ is six ticks to the right of $0$. On Line B, each tick is worth $0.1$. The value $0.45$ is halfway between $0.4$ and $0.5$, so mark it halfway between the fourth and fifth ticks after $0$.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Worked Solution: Retrieve and Connect 1, Question 4}
\small
\textbf{Question:} Sam says $P=4$, as it is the $4$th mark. What has he done wrong?
\awork{Solution: Sam counted the marks themselves, treating $0$ as the first mark. To find a value, count steps from $0$. Point $P$ is three steps from $0$, so $P=3$. It is the fourth mark, but its value is $3$.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Worked Solution: Retrieve and Connect 1, Question 5}
\small
\textbf{Question:} The chart shows $407.52$. What is the $4$ worth? And the $5$?
\awork{Solution: In $407.52$, the digit $4$ is in the hundreds column, so it is worth $4\times100=400$. The digit $5$ is in the tenths column, so it is worth $5\times0.1=0.5$.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Worked Solution: Retrieve and Connect 1, Question 6}
\small
\textbf{Question:} Work out $407.52\times 10$ and $407.52\div 10$.
\awork{Solution: Multiplying by $10$ moves every digit one place to the left, so $407.52\times10=4075.2$. Dividing by $10$ moves every digit one place to the right, so $407.52\div10=40.752$.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Worked Solution: Retrieve and Connect 1, Question 7}
\small
\textbf{Question:} A numberline goes $3.6$ to $3.7$ in $10$ equal steps. What is $3$ steps along from $3.6$?
\awork{Solution: The interval from $3.6$ to $3.7$ is $3.7-3.6=0.1$. Ten equal steps means each step is $0.1\div10=0.01$. Three steps give $3\times0.01=0.03$. Starting at $3.6$, the value is $3.6+0.03=3.63$.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Worked Solution: Retrieve and Connect 1, Question 8}
\small
\textbf{Question:} Use $7$, $5$, $4$, $2$ and a decimal point to make the largest number less than $100$.
\awork{Solution: To stay below $100$, use two digits before the decimal point. To make the number as large as possible, put the largest digit in the tens place: $7$. The largest remaining digit in the ones place is $5$, giving $75$. Then place the remaining digits $4$ and $2$ after the decimal point in descending order: $75.42$. Any number with three digits before the decimal point would be at least $100$, so $75.42$ is the largest.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Worked Solution: Retrieve and Connect 2, Question 1}
\hypertarget{work-st2}{}
\small
\textbf{Question:} Fill in $<$ or $>$: $7 \mathrel{\text{ }} 12$, $30 \mathrel{\text{ }} 3$.
\awork{Solution: On a number line, $7$ is to the left of $12$, so $7<12$. Also, $30$ is greater than $3$, so $30>3$.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Worked Solution: Retrieve and Connect 2, Question 2}
\small
\textbf{Question:} In $0.405$, what is the $4$ worth?
\awork{Solution: The digit $4$ is the first digit after the decimal point, so it is in the tenths column. It is worth $4$ tenths, which is $4/10=0.4$.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Worked Solution: Retrieve and Connect 2, Question 3}
\small
\textbf{Question:} On the place value chart, which is bigger, $0.4$ or $0.35$? Why?
\awork{Solution: Write $0.4$ as $0.40$ so both numbers have the same number of decimal places. In $0.40$, the tenths digit is $4$; in $0.35$, the tenths digit is $3$. Since $4$ tenths is greater than $3$ tenths, $0.4>0.35$.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Worked Solution: Retrieve and Connect 2, Question 4}
\small
\textbf{Question:} Fill in $<$, $>$ or $=$: $0.4 \mathrel{\text{ }} 0.40$, $0.6 \mathrel{\text{ }} 0.06$.
\awork{Solution: $0.4=0.40$ because the extra zero adds no value. Also, $0.6=6$ tenths $=60$ hundredths, while $0.06=6$ hundredths. Therefore $0.6>0.06$.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Worked Solution: Retrieve and Connect 2, Question 5}
\small
\textbf{Question:} Mark $0.38$ on Line A. Is it nearer $0.3$ or $0.4$?
\awork{Solution: Line A runs from $0.3$ to $0.4$ in hundredths. The number $0.38$ is $0.08$ above $0.3$ and $0.02$ below $0.4$. Since $0.02<0.08$, it is nearer to $0.4$.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Worked Solution: Retrieve and Connect 2, Question 6}
\small
\textbf{Question:} Order, smallest first: $0.5$, $0.45$, $0.405$, $0.54$.
\awork{Solution: Compare the tenths digits first. $0.405$ and $0.45$ are less than $0.5$ and $0.54$. For $0.405$ and $0.45$, compare hundredths: $0.405=0.405$ and $0.45=0.450$, so $0.405<0.45$. Also, $0.5=0.50$, so $0.5<0.54$. The order is $0.405$, $0.45$, $0.5$, $0.54$.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Worked Solution: Retrieve and Connect 2, Question 7}
\small
\textbf{Question:} True or false: more digits after the decimal point means a bigger number? Give an example/counter-example.
\awork{Solution: False. More digits after the decimal point do not automatically make a number larger. For example, $0.9$ has one decimal digit and $0.1234$ has four, but $0.9=0.9000$ is greater than $0.1234$. So $0.9>0.1234$.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Worked Solution: Retrieve and Connect 2, Question 8}
\small
\textbf{Question:} Alfie says $0.45>0.5$ as $45>5$. Explain his mistake, then write a decimal between $0.4$ and $0.405$.
\awork{Solution: Alfie compared the whole numbers $45$ and $5$, ignoring place value. $0.45$ means $45$ hundredths, while $0.5$ means $5$ tenths $=50$ hundredths. Since $50>45$, $0.5>0.45$. A decimal between $0.4=0.400$ and $0.405$ is $0.402$, for example.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Worked Solution: Retrieve and Connect 3, Question 1}
\hypertarget{work-st3}{}
\small
\textbf{Question:} Which is colder, $4\,^\circ$C or $-4\,^\circ$C?
\awork{Solution: Temperatures below $0^\circ$C are colder. The temperature $-4^\circ$C is below zero, while $4^\circ$C is above zero. Therefore $-4^\circ$C is colder.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Worked Solution: Retrieve and Connect 3, Question 2}
\small
\textbf{Question:} What is each mark on the thermometer worth? Write the temperatures at A and B.
\awork{Solution: The thermometer goes from $-10^\circ$C to $10^\circ$C in $10$ equal intervals, so each mark is $(10-(-10))\div10=20\div10=2^\circ$C. A is $3$ marks above $-10$, so A $=-10+3\times2=-6^\circ$C. B is $7$ marks above $-10$, so B $=-10+7\times2=4^\circ$C.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Worked Solution: Retrieve and Connect 3, Question 3}
\small
\textbf{Question:} Which is colder, $-3$ or $-8$? Fill in: $-3 \mathrel{\text{ }} -8$.
\awork{Solution: On a number line, $-8$ is to the left of $-3$, so $-8$ is colder and $-8<-3$. Therefore $-3>-8$. The missing symbol is $>$.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Worked Solution: Retrieve and Connect 3, Question 4}
\small
\textbf{Question:} Order, smallest first: $-2$, $5$, $-7$, $0$, $-1$.
\awork{Solution: Negative numbers are smaller than $0$ and positive numbers. Among the negatives, the most negative is smallest: $-7$, then $-2$, then $-1$. After that comes $0$, then $5$. The order is $-7$, $-2$, $-1$, $0$, $5$.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Worked Solution: Retrieve and Connect 3, Question 5}
\small
\textbf{Question:} Mark $-0.5$ and $-0.05$ on Line C. Which is larger?
\awork{Solution: Line C runs from $-1$ to $0$ in tenths. The value $-0.5$ is halfway between $-1$ and $0$. The value $-0.05$ is halfway between $0$ and $-0.1$, so it is very close to $0$. Numbers increase to the right, and $-0.05$ is to the right of $-0.5$. Therefore $-0.05>-0.5$, so $-0.05$ is larger.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Worked Solution: Retrieve and Connect 5, Question 4}

\textbf{Question:} Round to $1$ d.p.: $4.62$, $0.95$. Why is $0.95$ not $1$?
\awork{Solution: $4.62$: the hundredths digit is $2$, which is less than $5$, so $4.62\to4.6$. $0.95$: the hundredths digit is $5$, so round the tenths digit $9$ up. \begin{frame}{Worked Solution: Retrieve and Connect 5, Question 5}

\textbf{Question:} Round $12.348$ to $2$ d.p., then to $1$ d.p. Why not $12.4$?
\awork{Solution: To $2$ d.p., look at the thousandths digit $8$. Since $8\geq5$, round the hundredths digit $4$ up to $5$: $12.348\to12.35$. \begin{frame}{Worked Solution: Retrieve and Connect 5, Question 8}
\small
\textbf{Question:} (Challenge) Which listed decimals never settle into a repeating block? Can one still be a fraction?
\awork{Solution: The decimals that never settle into a repeating block are the non-repeating, never-ending ones: $\pi=3.14159265\ldots$, $\sqrt{2}=1.41421356\ldots$, and $0.1010010001\ldots$.
